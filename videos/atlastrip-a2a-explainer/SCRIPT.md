# SCRIPT — atlastrip-a2a-explainer

**Voice:** OpenAI `gpt-4o-mini-tts`, voice `ash`
**Voice direction:** A senior engineer walking a colleague through something they
built, in a meeting room, without slides. Warm, unhurried, faintly amused in the
middle. Land the numbers; do not sell them. Real pauses at the turns, especially
before "says no" and before "stops".

---

## Line 1 — The ask (Frame 1)

**Time:** 0.0 - 9.0s
**Delivery:** Set the scene. Ordinary, concrete, no drama yet.

    A robotics company needs one engineer in Tokyo next month, for a customer
    review. Simple ask. Four separate decisions.

## Line 2 — The four decisions (Frame 2)

**Time:** 9.0 - 18.0s
**Delivery:** Four beats, evenly weighted. This is the business problem.

    What she flies. Where she sleeps. Whether the policy allows it.
    And who signs for it.

## Line 3 — The team (Frame 3)

**Time:** 18.0 - 33.0s
**Delivery:** Introduce them like colleagues. Slight warmth on each name.

    So there are five of them. Skyline books the air. Hearth finds the room.
    Sentinel knows the rulebook. Ledger holds the money. And the Concierge takes
    the request and runs the desk.

## Line 4 — The catch (Frame 4)

**Time:** 33.0 - 48.0s
**Delivery:** This is the turn. Dry, matter of fact, land "will not install".

    Here is the catch. They were not built by the same team, and they do not run
    on the same stack. LangGraph. Google's ADK. CrewAI. LlamaIndex. Pydantic AI.
    Put all five in one Python process and it will not even install.

## Line 5 — Two protocols (Frame 5)

**Time:** 48.0 - 61.0s
**Delivery:** The answer. Two clean halves, a real gap between them.

    So they do not share a process. They share two protocols. M C P is how an
    agent reaches its tools. A2A is how one agent hands work to another. Not a
    function call. A task, with a life of its own.

## Line 6 — The request (Frame 6)

**Time:** 61.0 - 69.0s
**Delivery:** Lift the pace. The story starts moving.

    The request arrives in plain English. The Concierge reads it, and puts two
    agents to work at the same time.

## Line 7 — What came back (Frame 7)

**Time:** 69.0 - 80.0s
**Delivery:** Report it. Small lift on the last number; it is about to matter.

    Skyline comes back with United, premium economy, three thousand one hundred
    and ten dollars. Hearth comes back with a hotel two hundred metres from the
    customer's door, at two hundred and ninety-eight a night.

## Line 8 — Sentinel says no (Frame 8)

**Time:** 80.0 - 97.0s
**Delivery:** The best passage. Beat before "says no". Longer beat before "does
not do".

    Sentinel reads that, and says no. Policy caps Tokyo at two hundred and
    eighty. Now watch what the Concierge does not do. It does not overrule
    Sentinel. It does not overrule Hearth. It asks Hearth again, with the cap as
    a hard limit.

## Line 9 — The second answer (Frame 9)

**Time:** 97.0 - 105.0s
**Delivery:** Quick, satisfied. The system worked.

    A hundred and eighty-nine a night, five hundred metres out. Sentinel clears
    it.

## Line 10 — Ledger stops (Frame 10)

**Time:** 105.0 - 119.0s
**Delivery:** Slow down. Full stop after "stops". Let "and waits" sit.

    Then Ledger. Three thousand six hundred and eighty-eight dollars, against
    seven thousand six hundred left this quarter. It fits. But it is over the
    line where a human has to sign. So Ledger stops. Not fails. Stops, and
    waits.

## Line 11 — The pause spreads (Frame 11)

**Time:** 119.0 - 130.0s
**Delivery:** Quietly pleased. This is the payoff of the whole design.

    And the waiting spreads. The Concierge pauses too, and the question surfaces
    to the one person who can answer it. She approves. Both agents pick up
    exactly where they stopped. The trip is booked.

## Line 12 — The close (Frame 12)

**Time:** 130.0 - 140.0s
**Delivery:** Land it and stop. No upsell.

    Five specialists. Five frameworks. One conversation. Nobody is in charge of
    everybody. They just agreed on how to talk.
